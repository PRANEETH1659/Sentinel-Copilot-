Runbook: Ransomware Response
Owner: Security Operations Team
Last Reviewed: 2026-02-02

Purpose:
Defines immediate actions when ransomware activity is suspected or confirmed
on any company-owned endpoint or server.

Step 1 - Isolate Immediately:
Disconnect the affected host from the network (unplug ethernet or disable
Wi-Fi) but do NOT power it off. Powering off can destroy volatile memory
evidence needed for forensic analysis and may trigger destructive payloads
in some ransomware families.

Step 2 - Identify Scope:
- Check EDR telemetry for lateral movement indicators from the patient-zero
  host: unusual SMB traffic, new scheduled tasks, or suspicious use of admin
  tools like PsExec or WMI.
- Identify which file shares or backup systems the compromised account had
  access to, since ransomware often targets backups first.

Step 3 - Contain:
- Disable the compromised user account(s) at the identity provider.
- Isolate any additional hosts showing lateral movement indicators.
- Block identified command-and-control IPs/domains at the firewall.
- Preserve, do not delete, encrypted files - they may be needed for a
  decryption tool if one becomes available, and are required for forensics.

Step 4 - Assess and Decide:
- Determine backup integrity: are recent, uninfected backups available?
- Engage legal and executive leadership before any decision involving
  ransom payment; this is a business and legal decision, never made
  unilaterally by the security team.
- Engage external incident response support if internal capacity is
  insufficient for the scope of the incident.

Step 5 - Recovery:
- Rebuild affected systems from known-clean images rather than attempting
  to "clean" a compromised host.
- Restore data from verified clean backups only.
- Rotate all credentials that were accessible from compromised systems,
  including service accounts and API keys.

Step 6 - Post-Incident:
- Conduct a full root-cause analysis: how did the initial access occur
  (phishing, exposed RDP, unpatched vulnerability)?
- Update detection rules based on the specific indicators observed.
- File a formal incident report; ransomware incidents typically require
  notification to regulators or affected customers depending on data
  exposure.
